## 2. What V15 Was Trying to Learn

V14 suggested, only after its Hidden reveal, that a failed breakout might behave differently on a first clean boundary test versus repeated pressure. V15 deliberately tested that hypothesis on untouched data instead of assuming it was true.

The result was a clean falsification:
- V14-style base fade on V15 Study: **11/23 = 47.83%**.
- Zero prior same-edge attacks over the recent 8 bars: **4/9 = 44.44%**.
- Flipping repeated attacks to continuation: best broad variants only around **56.5%**.
- Accepted breakout continuation: about **50%**.

So the V14 observation did **not** generalize. This is a positive research result even though it did not produce a tradable rule.

## 4. New Study Family: Local Impulse → Counter-Pullback → Resume

The only broad family that showed a meaningful Study edge was a local, not purely higher-timeframe, continuation sequence:

1. A local directional **impulse** occurs.
2. Price makes a measurable **counter-pullback**.
3. If there is no strong 4H trend directly opposing the original impulse, predict the next 15m candle back in the impulse direction.

A parameter neighborhood around impulse 1.25–1.75 ATR and pullback 0.2–0.5 ATR produced roughly mid/high-60s, with enough signals to avoid selecting a tiny sample.

## 6. Frozen Official V15 Rule

### 6.1 Local impulse
On completed 15m candles, at signal candle `i`:
- `ATR = ATR(7)` on 15m.
- Impulse displacement = `Close[i-3] - Close[i-8]`.
- Direction `d = sign(Impulse)`.
- Require `abs(Impulse) >= 1.25 × ATR7`.

This measures a five-candle local impulse ending three 15m candles before the signal.

### 6.2 Counter-pullback
- Pullback displacement = `Close[i] - Close[i-3]`.
- Pullback direction must be opposite `d`.
- Require `0.50 <= abs(Pullback)/ATR7 <= 1.50`.

### 6.3 4H opposite-direction veto
Using the latest completed native Binance 4H candle:
- strong UPTREND if `ER(6) >= 0.30`, `EMA5 > EMA13`, and EMA13 3-bar slope > 0;
- strong DOWNTREND if `ER(6) >= 0.30`, `EMA5 < EMA13`, and EMA13 3-bar slope < 0.

If that strong 4H trend points **opposite** the local impulse direction, the setup becomes **NO TRADE**.

### 6.4 Prediction / expiry
Otherwise predict the **next 15m candle in the original impulse direction**.
- exactly one-candle expiry;
- no mandatory 5m confirmation;
- no cooldown;
- no D1 filter;
- no failed-breakout branch;
- all other states = `NO TRADE`.

This cannot justify selecting `1.00 ATR` after the fact. The valid learning is broader:

## 20. What Did NOT Improve the Edge

V15 also gives several useful negative results:
- Increasing impulse strength did **not** improve OOS accuracy; 1.5–1.75 ATR variants were worse.
- Very large impulses can be late/exhausted rather than higher confidence.
- Requiring the 4H trend to align with the local impulse was harmful in Hidden: **6/19 = 31.58%**.
- 4H-neutral cases were better at **15/28 = 53.57%**, but still not a reliable edge.
- Volatility-ratio thresholds did not rescue the rule.
- Cooldowns did not rescue it.
- Waiting for the signal candle body to turn back with the impulse did not help.

This directly challenges the simplistic belief that “more momentum + more HTF alignment = stronger continuation.”

## 21. Scientific Interpretation

V15 materially improved the research model despite failing OOS:

1. **V14's first-clean-rejection hypothesis was falsified** on untouched Study data.
2. Repeated boundary pressure was not reliably fade or continuation by itself.
3. Accepted breakouts were approximately random over the next 15m close.
4. A local impulse/pullback family showed a real Study plateau, but its official version failed OOS.
5. **Impulse magnitude was not the missing variable.** Bigger impulses did not help and sometimes hurt.
6. **Reset depth/quality is now the stronger candidate state variable.**
7. Higher-timeframe alignment can be stale: a strong 4H trend may describe the broader direction while the next 15m is already in rebound/exhaustion phase.

The emerging distinction is no longer merely “trend vs range.” It is closer to:

**fresh impulse → sufficient reset → re-acceleration** versus **impulse → shallow pause → noisy/late continuation attempt**.
