# 2. Full V1–V18 Learning Review Before V19

## V1 — Fixed bias failure

### Error
The model overweighted bearish moving-average structure and treated resistance as too permanent.

### Why it failed
Accepted price action and breakout continuation changed the regime faster than lagging indicators.

### Correct lesson
**Trigger → Confirmation → Invalidation → Flip → Reassessment**

### Permanent KEEP
Never defend the original thesis after structural invalidation.

---

## V2 — Momentum magnitude ≠ confidence

### Error
Large 4H momentum was treated as stronger continuation confidence.

### Why it failed
Large movement can represent exhaustion or range-edge expansion rather than continuation.

### Correct lesson
**Regime before direction.**

### Permanent KEEP
`NO TRADE` is a legitimate output.

---

## V3 — Regime without expiry

### Error
Once a 4H regime was classified, it remained active too long.

### Why it failed
A shock can invalidate an otherwise correct regime label.

### Correct lesson
A regime requires:
- entry,
- validation,
- expiry,
- reset.

---

## V4 — Reversal appearance mistaken for reversal state

### Error
Oversold conditions, wicks and low-timeframe reversal behavior were given too much authority.

### Why it failed
Breakout/repricing can continue through apparent exhaustion.

### Correct lesson
**Structure > oscillator.**

MFE/MAE must accompany directional accuracy.

---

## V5 — Visual rejection without failure of acceptance

### Error
Outer rejection setups continued fading an active repricing market.

### Why it failed
A wick is not a failed breakout.

### Correct lesson
**Failure of acceptance > appearance of rejection.**

---

## V6 — Borderline trend quality

### Error
A barely qualifying higher-timeframe trend was treated like a strong trend.

### Why it failed
Borderline trend can really be transition.

### Correct lesson
**Borderline trend = transition until proven otherwise.**

V6 also showed that trend → pullback → resume was a more coherent family than indiscriminate fading.
