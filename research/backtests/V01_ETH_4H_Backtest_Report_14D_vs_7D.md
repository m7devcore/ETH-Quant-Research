# ETH 4H Backtest Report — 14-Day Analysis vs 7-Day Forward Validation

## Purpose

This report documents a blind-style backtest on **ETHUSDT Futures using 4-hour candles**.

The objective was to:

1. Analyze only the historical data available during the first 14-day period.
2. Form a market outlook without using the following 7 days.
3. Reveal the next 7 days afterward.
4. Compare the forecast with what actually happened.
5. Identify what was correct, what was wrong, and what should be improved in future trading analysis.

---

## Test Structure

### Analysis Period

**14 days:** approximately **3 September 2026 → 17 September 2026**

Only data from this period was used to construct the original market thesis.

### Forward Validation Period

**7 days:** approximately **17 September 2026 → 24 September 2026**

These candles were treated as unseen during the first-stage analysis and were checked only after the forecast was established.

### Market

- Asset: **ETHUSDT**
- Venue: **Binance Futures**
- Timeframe: **4H**

# Part 4 — What Was Correct

## 1. Initial Rebound Was Identified Correctly

The forecast expected ETH to rebound from around **$2,443** toward the **$2,475–$2,500+ region**.

That happened.

During the first part of the test, ETH reached approximately:

**$2,497.77**

So the immediate rebound thesis was directionally correct.

---

## 2. The $2,420 Area Worked as Support

The analysis identified approximately **$2,420** as an important support zone.

The actual minimum during the 7-day forward period was:

**$2,426.52**

This support estimate was therefore close to the actual market floor during the test.

---

## 3. $2,525 Was Correctly Identified as an Invalidation / Regime-Change Area

This was the most useful part of the original analysis.

The thesis explicitly stated that sustained 4H acceptance above approximately **$2,525** should invalidate or materially weaken the bearish expectation.

That is exactly what became necessary.

Once ETH broke above that area, the market accelerated toward:

- $2,583
- $2,646
- $2,708
- eventually $2,806

This means the invalidation framework was more valuable than the original directional bias.

# Part 5 — What Was Wrong

## 1. The Main Weekly Direction Was Wrong

The most important error was the overall directional expectation.

The primary scenario expected:

**Rebound → resistance rejection → renewed downside**

Actual result:

**Breakout → continuation → approximately +10.18% over the test period**

Therefore, the main directional thesis failed.

---

## 2. Too Much Weight Was Given to the Previous Bearish Trend

The moving-average structure was:

**EMA9 < EMA21 < EMA50**

This was interpreted too heavily as evidence that the rebound would likely remain corrective.

The actual market was transitioning into a momentum reversal rather than merely producing a temporary relief bounce.

### Lesson

A bearish moving-average structure should not be treated as sufficient evidence that every rebound must fail.

Price acceptance, momentum expansion, breakout quality, and follow-through must be allowed to override the previous trend structure.

---

## 3. The $2,500–$2,525 Zone Was Framed Too Much as Resistance

The zone was correctly identified as important, but the interpretation should have been more conditional.

Better framework:

- **Rejection below the zone = bearish**
- **Strong 4H close above the zone = bearish thesis invalidated**
- **Acceptance above the zone = potential bullish regime shift**

The level itself was useful.

The main problem was the original bias attached to it.

---

## 4. The Forecast Expected Too Deep a Retest

The analysis considered possible downside tests toward:

- $2,390
- $2,360

These did not occur.

The actual low of the forward period was:

**$2,426.52**

This indicates that buyer strength was underestimated.

---

## 5. The Breakout Extension Was Underestimated

Even the alternative bullish scenario only anticipated approximately:

**$2,550–$2,615**

The actual high was:

**$2,806.76**

Once the breakout became established, the analysis did not allow enough room for trend expansion.

### Lesson

When a market breaks a major invalidation level with strong continuation, targets should not remain anchored to the old regime.

The analysis must re-measure the market after the breakout.

# Part 7 — Main Lessons

## Lesson 1 — Never Defend a Forecast Against New Price Information

A market forecast is conditional.

If the market breaks the level that invalidates the thesis, the thesis must be abandoned.

The objective is not to prove the original analysis correct.

The objective is to react correctly to new information.

---

## Lesson 2 — Use Trigger + Invalidation + Flip

Future analysis should not be expressed only as:

- Call
- Put
- Bullish
- Bearish

Instead, every setup should contain:

### Trigger

The condition required before entering or changing bias.

### Invalidation

The price action that proves the setup is no longer valid.

### Flip

The condition that changes the market bias completely.

---

## Lesson 3 — Separate Resistance From Regime Change

A major level can act as resistance before the breakout and become the trigger for a new trend after the breakout.

Example framework from this test:

**Below $2,525:** cautious / bearish bias remained reasonable.

**Confirmed 4H acceptance above $2,525:** bearish thesis invalidated.

**Sustained trade above $2,550:** bullish transition.

**Break above $2,615:** stop trying to repeatedly short the move; treat it as a potential trend breakout.

---

## Lesson 4 — Do Not Overweight Lagging Indicators

Moving averages are useful for market structure, but they are lagging.

They should not override:

- confirmed price breakout
- strong candle closes
- momentum expansion
- structural higher highs / higher lows
- volume expansion
- failure of bearish continuation

---

## Lesson 5 — Recalculate After a Breakout

Once the original market regime is invalidated, do not continue using targets derived from the old regime.

A new analysis should begin from the breakout structure.

# Part 8 — Trading Analysis Protocol Going Forward

For future ETH analysis, use this structure:

## 1. Market Context

Evaluate:

- higher-timeframe structure
- recent 4H trend
- support / resistance
- volatility
- momentum
- volume
- recent breakout or breakdown attempts

---

## 2. Primary Scenario

State:

- expected direction
- trigger
- entry zone
- first target
- secondary target
- invalidation

---

## 3. Opposite Scenario

Always define what would prove the primary thesis wrong.

Example:

> If price closes and holds above X, abandon the bearish thesis and reassess for a bullish continuation setup.

---

## 4. No-Trade Zone

Define the area where price action is too unclear to justify directional exposure.

---

## 5. Reassessment Rule

After every meaningful 4H structural break:

**Do not defend the previous forecast. Re-run the analysis using the new information.**

---

# Part 9 — Standard for Future Backtests

Any future backtest should follow the same anti-hindsight procedure:

1. Choose a historical cutoff date.
2. Hide all candles after that cutoff.
3. Analyze only the data available before the cutoff.
4. Write the forecast before revealing future candles.
5. Define:
   - direction
   - targets
   - support
   - resistance
   - invalidation
   - alternative scenario
6. Reveal the next period.
7. Compare forecast vs actual results.
8. Score the analysis.
9. Document every mistake.
10. Add the lesson to the future trading framework.
