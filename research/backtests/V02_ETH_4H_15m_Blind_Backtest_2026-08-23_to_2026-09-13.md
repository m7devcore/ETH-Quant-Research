---

# 1. Anti-Hindsight Protocol

This test was executed in two separate stages.

### Stage A — visible data
Only **23 August 2026 through 5 September 2026** was used to:
- inspect 4H and 15m structure,
- evaluate simple directional rules,
- form a conditional market thesis,
- freeze the directional rules before looking at 6–13 September.

### Stage B — hidden data
Only after the rules were frozen was **6 September through 13 September 2026** revealed.

No result from the hidden period was used to choose the original frozen rule.

---

# 2. Lessons Imported From the Previous MD Report

The previous backtest scored **35/100** and identified several important errors:
- over-weighting the previous bearish moving-average structure,
- treating resistance too much as a fixed rejection point,
- underestimating breakout extension,
- keeping targets anchored to an old regime,
- failing to let new price acceptance override the previous thesis.

The most important inherited framework was:

**Trigger → Confirmation → Invalidation → Flip → Reassessment**

This test therefore deliberately reduced the role of lagging moving averages and gave more importance to:
- price movement,
- breakout acceptance,
- higher-high / lower-low behavior,
- candle momentum,
- volume expansion,
- failure of continuation,
- separate logic for 4H and 15m.

# 16. What Was Wrong

## 16.1 The 4H rule was overfit to a tiny sample
The selected 4H continuation rule looked strong in the study sample:

**63.64%**

But that comparison contained only **33 post-warmup observations**.

In the hidden period it collapsed to:

**31.91%**

This is classic regime/sample instability.

### Correct lesson
A small in-sample edge must not be promoted directly into a live directional rule.

---

## 16.2 Stronger momentum produced worse results
The model assumed:

**larger 12-hour momentum = higher continuation confidence**

The hidden data showed the opposite.

The highest momentum-magnitude quartile scored:

**0 / 11 correct**

This is the single biggest analytical error in the test.

### Correct lesson
Momentum magnitude must be interpreted together with:
- range vs trend regime,
- distance from established support/resistance,
- wick structure,
- breakout acceptance,
- follow-through after the breakout candle.

A large move near an extreme can be exhaustion, not confirmation.

---

## 16.3 The 15m “edge” was not real enough
Study:
**53.98%**

Hidden:
**49.87%**

The difference is only a few percentage points, but that is the entire issue: a tiny apparent edge can disappear completely out of sample.

### Correct lesson
Do not predict every 15m candle.

A useful trading model should be allowed to say:

**NO TRADE / NO EDGE**

rather than forcing UP or DOWN 96 times per day.

---

## 16.4 Confidence was badly calibrated
On 4H:
- all-signal accuracy: **31.91%**
- high-confidence accuracy: **17.65%**

Confidence made the result worse.

A good confidence score should separate stronger from weaker setups.

This one did the opposite.

### Correct lesson
Confidence must be validated out of sample.  
If higher confidence does not produce better forward accuracy, it is not confidence — it is merely a larger indicator reading.

---

## 16.5 The model did not contain a true regime gate
The hidden week behaved primarily like:
- rotation / mean reversion,
- failed continuation,
- one abnormal volatility expansion,
- then rejection / retracement.

The frozen 4H rule always treated 12-hour momentum as continuation.

It had no mechanism to ask first:

**Is this market currently trending, ranging, or in a volatility shock?**

That missing decision was more important than the direction indicator itself.

---

# 17. What Should Have Been Done Instead

The post-mortem suggests that the next version should not begin with “UP or DOWN?”

It should begin with:

## Step 1 — Regime classification
Classify 4H into:
- TREND
- RANGE
- BREAKOUT ATTEMPT
- FAILED BREAKOUT
- VOLATILITY SHOCK

Only after that should directional logic be chosen.

---

## Step 2 — Acceptance rule
A breakout should require more than a wick.

A stronger version should require evidence such as:
- a 4H close beyond the structural level,
- limited rejection wick,
- follow-through / hold on the next candle,
- no immediate close back inside the prior range.

The 11 September $2,666 wick is a perfect example of why.

---

## Step 3 — No-trade state
The model should be able to output:

**NO TRADE**

especially on 15m.

Forcing a direction on every candle reduced the test to roughly random classification.

---

## Step 4 — Do not fit confidence on the same sample
The study period should be split internally into:
- development segment,
- calibration segment,

or use expanding walk-forward validation.

Then only the final untouched period should be used for the true score.

---

## Step 5 — Separate “forecast accuracy” from “trade quality”
A correct candle direction can still be a bad trade if:
- the movement is too small,
- spread/fees/slippage absorb the move,
- the adverse excursion is too large.

Future backtests should record:
- MAE,
- MFE,
- move size,
- fees,
- stop distance,
- target distance,
- expected value.

# 20. Permanent Lessons Added to the Trading Framework

1. **Do not convert a small-sample in-sample edge into a trading rule.**
2. **4H momentum can mean continuation in one regime and exhaustion in another.**
3. **A large wick is not breakout acceptance.**
4. **Higher indicator magnitude is not automatically higher confidence.**
5. **15m candle-by-candle direction is too noisy to force a trade every interval.**
6. **NO TRADE is a valid and necessary output.**
7. **Regime → Trigger → Confirmation → Invalidation → Flip → Reassessment**
8. **Confidence must improve out-of-sample accuracy or it is not calibrated confidence.**
9. **After a volatility shock, rebuild the structure; do not continue using the pre-shock rule unchanged.**
10. **Future tests must include trade-quality metrics, not only directional accuracy.**
